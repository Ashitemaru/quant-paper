\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models.} We evaluate six frontier models:
\ModelClaudeOpus, \ModelAstra, \ModelGemini, \ModelDeepSeek, \ModelGLM, and~\ModelKimi. All models run under default provider settings unless stated otherwise. Calls go through the platform gateway, which records token usage.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Each model enters the one-shot and iterative-research tracks with the platform harnesses (Section~\ref{sec:tracks}), on the suite of Section~\ref{sec:data}. Following the leaderboard rule, each model has \emph{one official run per track}, and Table~\ref{tab:main} reports that run. Additional attempts and failures are reported separately in Section~\ref{sec:reliability}.

\paragraph{Reference.} As an upper reference we include \emph{Navers}, a professional cross-sectional strategy developed by our team. It is evaluated by the same platform, on the same universe, windows, cash account, and scoring rule, using own-agent weights and zero model tokens. Navers is not an LLM entry. It shows how far a strong human-built strategy gets under identical conditions.

Table~\ref{tab:main} and Figure~\ref{fig:main} summarize the results.

\paragraph{No model comes close to the reference.} Navers scores 51.57 and returns 70.9\% in 2024. The best model, \ModelClaudeOpus\ with iterative research, scores 31.94 and returns 30.4\%. The three strongest models (\ModelClaudeOpus, \ModelAstra, \ModelDeepSeek) lie within 2.4 points of each other, far below the reference. \ModelGemini\ and \ModelGLM\ trail at 10.22 and 4.05.

\paragraph{Beating the market is rare.} The equal-weight market returned 17.1\% in 2024. Only two of ten official runs beat it: \ModelClaudeOpus\ (+13.3 pp) and \ModelDeepSeek\ (+1.9 pp), both with iterative research. \ModelAstra\ earns a positive 16.4\% but still trails the market. Because the score rewards excess rather than absolute return, \ModelAstra's in-sample lead (36.73, the highest of any model) does not carry over to 2024.

\paragraph{The sealed year reorders models.} In-sample and out-of-sample rankings disagree. \ModelAstra\ ranks first in-sample but third out-of-sample, while \ModelGemini\ and \ModelGLM\ hold respectable in-sample scores (24.63 and 13.51) that collapse in 2024 (4.04 and 0.00). This is the gap the sealed window is designed to expose: research-window performance is an unreliable proxy for generalization.

\paragraph{Risk profiles differ.} \ModelDeepSeek's 2024 strategy has the highest Sharpe ratio (1.16) and by far the smallest drawdown ($-7.9\%$). It trades rarely and stays in cash on many days. In particular, it held cash through the February crash. \ModelClaudeOpus\ earns more but with a drawdown of $-31.1\%$. Figure~\ref{fig:nav} shows that for every model, most of the 2024 gain arrives in the market rally of late September. Before the rally, all model strategies except \ModelDeepSeek's were below their starting value, whereas Navers was already ahead of the market.

\subsection{Does Iterative Research Help?}
\label{sec:feedback_effect}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \figorph{Figures/fig_oneshot_vs_vanilla.pdf}{\linewidth}{One-shot vs iterative}
    \caption{One-shot versus iterative research for each model: 2024 excess return over the market (left) and total score (right).}
    \label{fig:oneshot_vs_vanilla}
\end{figure}

Every model scores higher with iterative research than with one-shot generation (Figure~\ref{fig:oneshot_vs_vanilla}). In the one-shot track, every strategy loses to the market in 2024, with excess returns between $-11.5$ and $-37.1$ percentage points. Four of five one-shot strategies score zero. Their signals carry essentially no predictive information out of sample: 2024 RankIC is between $-0.015$ and $0.022$. Only \ModelClaudeOpus\ scores at all (4.41), from its in-sample window.

With feedback, 2024 RankIC rises to 0.029--0.049 for every model, and every total score increases. Excess return improves for four models, by 7.5 (\ModelGLM) to 39.0 (\ModelDeepSeek) percentage points. \ModelGemini\ is the exception: its signal improves, but its 2024 excess return falls from $-15.6$ to $-19.9$ percentage points, an instance of the signal--portfolio gap discussed next. Iteration is also where tokens go: one-shot runs use 16--96k tokens, iterative runs 96--418k. Without feedback, current models do not produce usable quantitative strategies; with five rounds of feedback, they produce strategies with real but modest predictive power.

\subsection{Predictive Signal versus Portfolio Outcome}
\label{sec:signal_vs_portfolio}

Strong signal statistics do not guarantee a strong portfolio. In the iterative track, all five models reach similar 2024 RankIC (0.029--0.049), yet their 2024 excess returns span 38 percentage points, from $+13.3$ to $-24.9$. The two models with the \emph{highest} in-sample RankIC, \ModelGemini\ (0.064) and \ModelGLM\ (0.056), produce the \emph{worst} 2024 portfolios. Their long--short Sharpe ratios fall to zero or below. \ModelClaudeOpus\ has the lowest 2024 RankIC of the five (0.029) but the best portfolio and a long--short Sharpe of 1.51.

RankIC measures how well a strategy orders the whole market. The portfolio depends only on the top 75 stocks, and on whether they can actually be bought and held under price limits. A model that optimizes average correlation can improve the ordering of thousands of uninteresting stocks while leaving the head of the ranking no better than chance. This is why \BenchmarkName\ scores signal and portfolio terms jointly, and why the geometric mean in Equation~\eqref{eq:quality} requires every term to be positive.

\subsection{Granularity of Research Feedback}
\label{sec:e2}

The iterative track returns aggregate return, signal, and coverage statistics along with execution errors. To test how much this information matters, we ran a control suite that is identical except that feedback contains \emph{only execution errors}: the model learns whether its strategy ran, but not how it performed.

Under errors-only feedback, both tested models score zero. \ModelAstra's 2024 excess return falls from $-0.7$ to $-23.5$ percentage points, and \ModelDeepSeek's from $+1.9$ to $-20.2$. The behavior changes as well: without performance information, \ModelAstra\ stops after a single evaluation and uses 50k tokens instead of 96k. The value of iteration comes from performance feedback, not from the opportunity to fix bugs. \phtext{Additional models under errors-only feedback pending.}

\subsection{What Happens During Research}
\label{sec:process}

We compare the first executable candidate of each completed iterative run with the strategy it finally commits to. Eight of nine runs improve their in-sample excess return over that trajectory, by 10.6 percentage points on average. No run commits to its first candidate, and six commit to the candidate with the best in-sample result. Models therefore use feedback actively and select greedily on the research window. Because only the committed strategy is assessed on 2024, this improvement is in-sample progress. Section~\ref{sec:main} shows that it transfers unevenly.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \figorph{Figures/fig_tokens_vs_score.pdf}{\linewidth}{Tokens vs score}
    \caption{Tokens used versus total score for all completed runs. Official runs are highlighted.}
    \label{fig:tokens}
\end{figure}

Token use and score are only loosely related (Figure~\ref{fig:tokens}). \ModelAstra\ reaches 30.29 with 96k tokens, while \ModelGemini\ and \ModelGLM\ spend about 370k tokens for scores of 10.22 and 4.05. \ModelClaudeOpus\ and \ModelDeepSeek\ spend 320k and 418k for the top two out-of-sample scores. Spending more of the budget helps only when the model can turn feedback into better candidates.

\subsection{Reliability}
\label{sec:reliability}

Completing a research run is itself a capability. Table~\ref{tab:attempts} reports all attempts in the main campaign, excluding runs cancelled because of platform or provider incidents. Failure rates differ sharply by model. Here a failure means the run produced no valid committed strategy: an invalid proposal, a strategy that fails during execution, or a harness protocol failure.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \ModelAstra\ and \ModelDeepSeek\ completed every attempt.
    \item \ModelClaudeOpus\ failed 7 of 10 one-shot attempts.
    \item \ModelKimi\ failed 5 of 7 one-shot attempts and its only iterative attempt.
\end{itemize}
\ModelKimi's failures occurred through a third-party relay and its runs through the official API are pending. \ph{Failure breakdown by cause pending.} A model that cannot reliably produce an executable strategy in one pass has a lower expected score in practice than its best run suggests.
