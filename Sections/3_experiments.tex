\label{sec:experiments}

This section defines the comparison and reporting policy for model choice, research procedure, resource budget, and input profile. It contains no measured ranking or effect size. Campaign-specific values and empirical findings are left blank.

\label{sec:exp_setup}

Input-profile comparisons use separately identified suites with matched dates and financial rules where feasible. Calendar boundaries include the predecessor signal and final label realization required by Section~\ref{sec:arena_returns}. Data provenance, population construction, prior public availability, development use, and the method freeze point must be recorded before final evaluation.

Their original historical returns are not substituted for those observations, and they do not enter model-token efficiency comparisons.

\subsection{Leaderboard and Reporting Policy}
\label{sec:leaderboard}

\paragraph{Row retention and ranking.}
The main report retains every registered model--harness participant, including failed and incomplete episodes. Financial ranks require official assessment and the scorecard's financial eligibility. They order mean replicate Top75 research ARR within the same suite, budget, and preregistered coverage stratum; excluded rows retain their metrics and reasons without a rank. Coverage strata and any minimum evidence requirement belong to the paper's reporting policy and must be frozen before final outcomes are inspected. No cross-stratum order is assigned, and no rank is populated until this policy is registered. The platform's financial board does not itself impose this additional comparability rule.

Composite ranks are reported separately for generally eligible episodes with available composites under matching calibration, component weights, complete required token usage, and verified feature evidence when needed. The main report shows score availability for all participants, so this subset cannot be mistaken for a ranking of the entire field. The campaign export's model-ranking flag is not used as a substitute for either eligibility rule; Appendix~\ref{app:eligibility} gives the exact distinctions.

\paragraph{Financial evidence and coverage.}
Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} places cumulative research return $R^{75}$, ARR, paired excess ARR, and effective/expected cohort counts on the same reporting path. It also records panel-level signal, label, and paired coverage, selected-cohort label coverage, and valid IC days. The research-return endpoint compares observable selected labels under fixed exposure and costs; it does not require every selected security to be funded or filled. Cash-account outcomes and the Top200/400 diagnostics accompany the completed report. Research-period best scores are kept separate from the frozen strategy's final-window scorecard.

\paragraph{Completeness and uncertainty.}
Each row links to its episode and final scorecards and retains prescribed/completed replicate counts, execution status, failure reasons, metric sample counts, and dispersion. Complete aggregates require all prescribed observations. Any success-conditioned summary is labeled and accompanied by completion rates. Coverage counts and ratios are disclosed per replicate rather than pooled to hide a failed or sparse run. Missing measurements carry their reasons; token and CPU measurements each carry a completeness flag. Zero-diversity values retain feature-level reasons (Appendix~\ref{app:diversity}).

Dispersion across replicates measures variation in the model--harness procedure. Time-series uncertainty measures variation in the market observations and must account for overlapping cohorts; these are reported separately. Any moving-block bootstrap analysis registers its resampling unit, block length, resamples, and seed, with a justification for the time-dependence treatment.

\subsection{Benchmark Analyses}
\label{sec:exp_analysis}

\paragraph{Model and harness effects.}
One-shot and vanilla comparisons evaluate complete research modes. They differ jointly in feedback, model-assisted repair, training opportunities, and final-candidate selection. Their outcome difference therefore cannot identify a feedback-only effect. Component attribution requires matched training permissions, repair budgets, selection rules, and model resources, changing only the mechanism being studied. External harnesses enter the comparison only after their procedures and evaluation variants are registered.

\paragraph{Budget and information effects.}
Preregistered token and evaluation limits will be compared alongside observed usage and its completeness. Tokens required to reach a fixed research target may be reported as a secondary diagnostic, with non-attainment and incomplete execution distinguished. Input-profile comparisons disclose both information availability and preprocessing differences.

\paragraph{Financial sensitivity.}
Cost and coverage analyses will compare the same frozen strategies under explicitly versioned conditions. They will examine cumulative research returns alongside ARR, coverage strata, and funded-account outcomes. Cost variation distinguishes the research cohort's fixed deduction from account commissions and execution assumptions. Alternative settings preserve the original scorecard and do not replace missing labels with zero. These analyses are needed to determine how much a conclusion depends on sparse observations or the chosen execution convention.

\paragraph{Reference and operational checks.}
Fixed-factor references will be reevaluated on the target suite, retaining missingness and disclosing instrument-scope differences. Platform checks will exercise future-data rejection, research/final isolation, final-commitment immutability, recovery of accepted daily outputs, and unresolved model operations. Real-data agreement between research and Arena entry points requires matched factors, dates, releases, parameters, runtime versions, and complete replay records. Synthetic checks, deployed boundary checks, and campaign completion, recovery, and overhead measurements remain separate evidence classes.
