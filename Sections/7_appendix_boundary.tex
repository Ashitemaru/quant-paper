\section{Information Boundary and Qualification Rules}
\label{app:boundary}

\subsection{Authorization and Final-Assessment Boundary}

The model interaction service receives the public suite, authorized tools, permitted research feedback, and remaining-resource state. Quantitative strategy execution receives a separately scoped market-data capability. The trusted supervisor holds that capability and mediates requests through the permitted version, dates, instruments, fields, and availability cutoff. Strategy programs cannot expand the scope by supplying another date or forwarding their own internal authorization headers. Final evaluation uses the registered sealed release through the private data plane.

A final commitment ends candidate selection for that replicate. The same frozen strategy and trained artifact, when present, are used for sealed assessment. Recovery continues the recorded operation and accepted progress; it does not authorize a new candidate or new paid model response. A separate replay or changed evaluation contract creates a separate result and preserves the original commitment.

This boundary addresses runtime access and evaluation ownership. It does not establish that historical information was absent from a model's training data or prior resources. Organizers must document population construction, earlier publication of data, development experiments, and the freeze point. Public benchmark feedback can also influence later submissions. Campaign registration therefore specifies the intended experiment set and disclosure policy, retaining all attempts used for the reported comparison. Periodically refreshed or separately retained final windows are possible governance extensions, rather than assumed properties of the current runtime.

\subsection{Feature Diversity as an Evaluation Dimension}
\label{app:diversity}

The benchmark considers whether a strategy declares distinct, qualified features and actually uses them. Repeated copies of a feature do not constitute additional diversity. For the public research protocol, the feature count is capped at 32, with rank-based regression used to assess contribution and correlated features grouped before counting. These groups describe statistical redundancy; they do not establish independent economic mechanisms. The supplied campaign has no reference-bank novelty measurement.

The public research contribution enters Equation~\eqref{eq:efficiency}. Final-window feature qualification and its mapping into the final score are private. We disclose the evaluation dimension without publishing final qualification thresholds, component values, or an algorithm for reconstructing that score.

\subsection{Metric Availability and Score Eligibility}

Execution completion, strategy freezing, metric availability, and score eligibility are separate states. Eligibility does not establish full-window coverage or low risk. Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} therefore retains coverage qualifications alongside financial results. Missing metrics are not filled with zeros, and financial records with unavailable totals remain in the relevant financial summaries.

\paragraph{Verification and access.}
Public campaign records support checks of submission identity, outcome counts, resource use, and reported results. Exact trade scheduling, data-availability cutoffs, and final-score construction are held by the organizer. Hosted re-evaluation uses the registered evaluator version; the public description alone does not support independent reconstruction of the private final score.
