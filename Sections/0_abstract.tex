\abstract{%
Large language models (LLMs) increasingly participate in quantitative research by formulating market hypotheses, writing executable strategies, and refining them through empirical feedback. Evaluating this capability requires a common account of the data an agent can observe, the resources it can consume, and the evidence used to select and assess its final strategy. We introduce \textbf{\BenchmarkName}, a benchmark and execution platform for complete, budgeted quantitative research runs on real historical market data. The benchmark evaluates a language model together with its research harness under frozen input releases, chronological information boundaries, resource budgets, and sealed final assessment. Its daily protocol specifies signal availability, portfolio selection, overlapping holding periods, transaction-cost assumptions, and missing-observation treatment, while reporting research returns separately from simulated cash-account outcomes. The Arena platform separates research decisions from quantitative execution, mediates date-scoped data access, and preserves strategy commitments, resource accounting, and accepted progress across recoverable interruptions. This design supports controlled comparisons across models, research procedures, and input profiles, with explicit qualification rules for financial, efficiency, and diversity metrics. \BenchmarkName\ provides a system-level foundation for studying how effectively LLM agents convert limited research resources into out-of-sample signals.
}
