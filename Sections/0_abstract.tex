\abstract{%
Large language models (LLMs) increasingly participate in quantitative research by proposing market hypotheses, writing strategies, and interpreting experimental feedback. Evaluating this capability requires explicit rules for data access, resource use, and final-strategy assessment. We introduce \textbf{\BenchmarkName}, a benchmark and execution platform for budgeted research episodes on real historical market data, including failed attempts and final-strategy selection. The participant is a language model together with its research harness. A daily evaluation protocol specifies signal timing, portfolio selection, overlapping holding periods, costs, and missing observations. Research returns are reported with observation coverage and separately from simulated cash-account outcomes; financial ranking and composite-score availability have distinct requirements. The Arena platform enforces date-scoped access, records resource measurements and unresolved usage, and preserves committed strategies and accepted progress across recoverable interruptions. This paper presents the implemented protocol and its reporting rules; empirical rankings remain to be established. \BenchmarkName\ defines a reproducible protocol for evaluating budgeted strategy search on held-out historical data, with records that support reconstruction of observed research trajectories.
}
