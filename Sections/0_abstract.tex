\abstract{%
Large language models (LLMs) can propose market hypotheses, implement trading signals, and revise strategies using backtest feedback. Evaluating this process requires control over data access, search budgets, candidate selection, and final assessment. We introduce \textbf{\BenchmarkName}, a released benchmark for complete quantitative research runs on historical market data. Each run registers an interaction track and resource contract, then commits one strategy for sealed assessment. The benchmark reports predictive quality, portfolio performance, resource use, and execution outcomes separately. Its research-window scoring rule is public; final-score construction and exact execution timing remain private. We study a Shenzhen A-share pilot with 106 episodes, including 24 completed main-experiment strategies and four completed feedback controls. Offline cash-account re-evaluation yields positive returns for eight main-experiment strategies, of which two exceed the aligned gross market reference. Eight of nine completed iterative runs improve their research-window excess metric, but intermediate candidates have no final-window assessments. Higher predictive correlation also coexists with lower portfolio returns in one model comparison. These observations motivate separate evaluation of research progress, signal quality, and financial outcomes. The incomplete campaign and shared historical window limit conclusions about model rankings and generalization.
}
