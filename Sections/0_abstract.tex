\abstract{%
Quantitative research is an iterative optimization problem: a researcher proposes a hypothesis, writes an executable signal, evaluates it on historical data, and decides what to keep or abandon. For LLMs, evaluating this capability requires a benchmark that measures the complete run rather than a single formula or selected backtest. We introduce \textbf{\BenchmarkName}, a released benchmark for quantitative research on real historical market data. Each run fixes the data release, information boundary, interaction track, resource contract, strategy-freezing rule, financial protocol, and sealed final assessment. Evaluation considers predictive quality, signal stability, portfolio returns, risk-adjusted performance, turnover, resource use, and feature diversity. The research scoring rule is public; final-score construction and exact trade timing remain private. The primary analysis uses offline cash-account re-evaluation, with historical metrics retained separately. A Shenzhen A-share pilot contains 106 episodes, including 24 completed main-experiment strategies and four completed feedback controls. Eight main-experiment strategies earn positive account returns, but only two exceed an aligned gross market reference. Eight of nine completed iterative runs improve their research-window excess metric; higher predictive correlation nevertheless need not produce better final portfolio returns. These results expose the gap between progress during research and economic generalization, motivating evaluation of the complete research run and its intermediate evidence.
}
