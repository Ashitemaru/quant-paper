\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Quantitative research turns hypotheses about market behavior into executable trading strategies. Researchers construct features from market observations, combine them into predictive factors or signals, and use those signals to build and test portfolios. Large language models (LLMs) are a natural fit for this research loop: they can propose hypotheses, implement them in code, interpret feedback, and revise their approach. Recent systems already explore this potential through interactive alpha discovery and iterative factor search \citep{Wang2023AlphaGPT,Tang2025AlphaAgent,Han2026QuantaAlpha}.

Existing benchmarks measure different parts of this workflow. AlphaQT-Bench evaluates the correctness of quantitative trading code, while AlphaBench studies the generation, evaluation, and search of formulaic factors \citep{AlphaQTBench2026,Luo2026AlphaBench}. StockBench evaluates agents that make sequential trading decisions from market information \citep{StockBench2025}. AlphaForgeBench extends evaluation to generating factor and strategy code from natural-language objectives and backtesting it on historical daily market data \citep{Zhang2026AlphaForgeBench}.
To our knowledge, this is the first benchmark on real market data in which models construct features from raw order and trade records, revise their signals using bounded feedback, and submit a frozen strategy for out-of-sample assessment under common trading rules. This setting evaluates feature construction, signal combination, and strategy selection through their joint effect on the final portfolio.

We introduce \textbf{\BenchmarkName}, a benchmark for end-to-end quantitative research on real A-share market data (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). At the input end, model-generated Python programs access raw Level-2 records, including individual orders and trades and order-book snapshots, through a controlled data interface. Models determine which features to construct and how to combine them into daily stock scores. During research, they can evaluate candidate strategies on 2022--2023, inspect the permitted feedback, and revise their code within a fixed budget. Each run then commits one frozen strategy. At the output end, the platform executes that strategy on the sealed year 2024 and measures the portfolio's returns after trading costs.

\BenchmarkName{} applies common portfolio and execution rules to make these outcomes comparable. Daily scores determine an equal-weight portfolio of the highest-ranked stocks, and a cash-account evaluator applies lot-size constraints and order-dependent costs, including market impact estimated from a simulated order book. Out-of-sample return and excess return over the market provide direct measures of profitability. Risk and signal statistics and the diversity of features used by the strategy provide complementary views of the result; token use is reported but not scored. Together, these measurements distinguish finding a predictive signal from turning it into a profitable portfolio.

A common research budget controls how much search each run can undertake. Repeated backtests favor candidates that fit historical noise \citep{Bailey2017Backtest}, so the selected strategy must be assessed on data that its research never observed. The platform also records candidate evaluations, resource use, and final submissions. These records allow us to examine how models use feedback and select strategies alongside their eventual financial outcomes.

Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{An end-to-end quantitative research benchmark.} \BenchmarkName{} connects feature and signal construction from real market events to the out-of-sample performance of an executable strategy, making the complete research run the unit of evaluation.
    \item \textbf{A common research and financial evaluation protocol.} A bounded research process, frozen strategy submission, and sealed assessment under shared portfolio and trading rules support comparisons of profitability, risk, signal quality, resource use, and feature diversity.
    \item \textbf{An empirical analysis of LLM quantitative research.} We evaluate nine frontier models in one-shot and iterative settings. Iterative research raises the overall score for all nine, yet no model beats the equal-weight market in 2024. The stocks a model ranks highest typically underperform the market average, so a positive rank correlation does not become a profitable portfolio. One model learns to stay out of the market in downtrends and avoids the February 2024 crash, but still trails the market over the year, and higher reasoning effort does not reliably raise scores.
    \item \textbf{A public evaluation platform.} \BenchmarkName{} is released as a public arena that accepts submissions from any model or agent harness under the same protocol and sealed assessment.
\end{itemize}

The public leaderboard also lists Kepler-1, a proprietary strategy developed by Einsia AI.
