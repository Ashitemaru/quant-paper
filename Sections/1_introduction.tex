\section{Introduction}

Quantitative trading seeks to turn noisy market observations into signals and portfolios that generate reliable out-of-sample returns. Formulaic alphas provide an executable and interpretable representation of such signals \citep{Kakushadze2016Formulaic}. A quantitative research workflow repeatedly formulates a market hypothesis, translates it into a computational strategy, evaluates the resulting signal, and uses the outcome to choose what to test next. Financial data are noisy and non-stationary, the candidate space is large, and only a fraction of hypotheses remain useful beyond the observations that motivated them. Large language models (LLMs) are well suited to participating in this workflow because they combine hypothesis formation, code generation, and experiment analysis within an interactive system. Recent alpha-discovery methods illustrate the growing range of research procedures built around these capabilities \citep{Wang2023AlphaGPT,Tang2025AlphaAgent,Han2026QuantaAlpha}.

Evaluating an LLM research agent introduces three closely related challenges. First, a final strategy reflects both the underlying model and the harness that organizes its prompts, tools, memory, candidate revisions, and stopping decisions. Comparisons therefore require a clearly specified unit of evaluation. Second, historical market data support repeated adaptive experimentation, creating opportunities for selection bias and backtest overfitting \citep{Bailey2017Backtest}. The research feedback available to an agent must be separated from the evidence used to judge its frozen output. Third, operational choices affect the outcome: unequal model budgets, different data access, silent retries, and inconsistent return conventions can change the apparent ranking of otherwise comparable systems. A benchmark must make these choices part of the evaluation contract.

These challenges motivate a system-level question: how effectively can an LLM agent convert a fixed research budget into an executable strategy that retains predictive and economic utility on a sealed historical interval? Answering this question requires observing the complete research process, including unsuccessful attempts, resource consumption, and final selection. It also requires precise financial semantics. A cross-sectional signal statistic, a fixed-notional research return, and a funded account outcome measure different properties of a strategy; their interpretation depends on the information cutoff, holding period, missing data, and cost assumptions.

We introduce \textbf{\BenchmarkName}, a benchmark and execution platform for budgeted quantitative research on real historical market data. A participant consists of a registered language model and a research harness. The harness proposes executable stock-scoring strategies, receives authorized research feedback, and commits one final strategy per replicate. A frozen suite specifies the data releases, input profile, chronological windows, runtime, budgets, feedback policy, and evaluator. The platform then assesses the committed strategy on a sealed interval. This formulation admits one-shot generation, ordinary feedback iteration, and external research harnesses within a common evaluation boundary.

The benchmark is supported by an Arena platform that separates research decisions, quantitative execution, and trusted evaluation. Research harnesses access model and evaluation tools; strategy programs obtain market observations through date-scoped authorization; the evaluator applies the registered financial protocol. Immutable input releases and frozen strategy artifacts identify what was evaluated. Durable operation records distinguish reservations from measured usage and allow recoverable interruptions to resume accepted work without silently generating a new model response. The same financial evaluation core supports internal factor research and Arena assessments, while each setting retains its own access and disclosure policy.

The daily benchmark developed here uses raw-price observations for a registered Shenzhen A-share population. It fixes a signal--entry--exit alignment, Top-$K$ selection with complete boundary ties, half-notional overlapping cohorts, and an explicit cost convention. Financial outcomes are accompanied by coverage, predictive statistics, and actual resource observations. Optional quality, efficiency, and diversity scores have stated eligibility conditions, so an unavailable diagnostic remains distinguishable from a measured zero. The leaderboard and the participant-specific RSI Harness will be reported in their reserved sections once their configurations and run records are available.

The contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A benchmark for complete quantitative research runs.} We define the model--harness pair as the participant and fix its information, feedback, resource, selection, and final-assessment contract, supporting matched comparisons across research procedures.
    \item \textbf{An explicit financial evaluation protocol.} We formalize temporal alignment, portfolio selection, research returns, missing observations, and score qualification, with separate evidence for simulated account behavior.
    \item \textbf{An Arena execution platform.} We implement controlled market-data access, isolated strategy execution, durable budget accounting, final strategy commitment, and recoverable evaluation, together with campaign records for reproducible comparison.
\end{itemize}
