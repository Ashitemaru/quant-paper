\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Formulaic Alpha Mining with Language Models}

Formulaic alpha mining searches for executable expressions that transform market observations into predictive cross-sectional signals. Published formula libraries demonstrate the interpretability and deployability of this representation \citep{Kakushadze2016Formulaic}. Automated approaches then use evolutionary search or learned generators to explore expression spaces and combine the resulting factors \citep{Zhang2020AutoAlpha,Shi2024AlphaForge}. These explicit search spaces support reproducible execution, but they are combinatorial, highly exposed to noisy backtest feedback, and often optimized around a narrow factor statistic rather than the behavior of the final portfolio.

LLMs extend this paradigm by connecting economic hypotheses, symbolic construction, and empirical diagnosis. LLMFactor uses sequential knowledge-guided prompting to extract interpretable factors from financial information \citep{Wang2024LLMFactor}; Automate Strategy Finding with LLM combines multimodal market inputs with a multi-agent portfolio workflow \citep{Kou2024AutomateStrategy}; Alpha-GPT introduces interactive LLM-based alpha generation \citep{Wang2023AlphaGPT}; AlphaAgent regularizes generation for originality, hypothesis--factor alignment, and structural simplicity \citep{Tang2025AlphaAgent}; and QuantaAlpha treats a complete mining trajectory as the unit of evolution and reuses effective sub-trajectories through mutation and crossover \citep{Han2026QuantaAlpha}. These systems establish the value of structured, multi-stage alpha discovery beyond one-shot formula generation. \BenchmarkName\ provides a common contract for evaluating such research procedures as model--harness participants, with executable strategy outputs, explicit resource use, and a frozen final-assessment boundary.

\subsection{Self-Improving Agents and Recursive Search}

Memory and iterative feedback are central to self-improving LLM agents. General agent frameworks such as ReAct interleave reasoning with environment actions \citep{Yao2023ReAct}, while Reflexion and Self-Refine use textual feedback and iterative revision without changing model parameters \citep{Shinn2023Reflexion,Madaan2023SelfRefine}. Voyager demonstrates how an executable skill library can support open-ended, lifelong improvement \citep{Wang2023Voyager}. In finance, FinMem organizes market information with layered memory for trading decisions \citep{Yu2023FinMem}, QuantAgent couples an inner response-refinement loop with an outer knowledge-base update loop \citep{Wang2024QuantAgent}, FactorMiner retains successful patterns, failure constraints, and reusable skills \citep{Wang2026FactorMiner}, and QuantaAlpha preserves trajectory-level evidence for evolutionary refinement \citep{Han2026QuantaAlpha}. AQuA closes bounded loops in which validated experimental evidence guides subsequent proposals, whereas QuantEvolver converts quantitative evaluation into policy-update signals through reinforcement fine-tuning \citep{Guo2026AQuA,Zhang2026QuantEvolver}. Together, these approaches show that improvement can occur through memory retrieval, structured candidate evolution, recursive experimentation, or model-parameter updates.

\BenchmarkName\ treats these forms of adaptation as participant properties. The benchmark fixes the data boundary, resource definitions, feedback, and financial evaluator so that different adaptive procedures can be compared under stated conditions. The RSI Harness section is reserved for a concrete participant implementation.

\subsection{Quantitative Benchmarks and Evaluation Protocols}

Existing financial infrastructure serves several different evaluation goals. Qlib standardizes AI-oriented quantitative workflows, while FinRL provides reproducible reinforcement-learning environments and trading evaluations \citep{Yang2020Qlib,Liu2020FinRL}. Financial language benchmarks such as FinBen and Finance Agent Benchmark evaluate domain understanding, generation, forecasting, and real-world research tasks \citep{Xie2024FinBen,Bigeard2025FinanceAgent}; general agent benchmarks such as AgentBench and MLAgentBench evaluate multi-turn interaction, tool use, and iterative experimentation \citep{Liu2023AgentBench,Huang2023MLAgentBench}. AlphaBench focuses specifically on formulaic alpha generation, evaluation, and search tasks \citep{Luo2026AlphaBench}, while AlphaForgeBench targets reproducible end-to-end factor and strategy design with LLMs \citep{Zhang2026AlphaForgeBench}. Recent trading-agent evaluation also emphasizes leakage control, attribution, and memory effects in historical markets \citep{Zhu2026KTDFin}. End-to-end trading agents add planning, memory, and execution, but their realized returns often combine factor discovery with timing, portfolio construction, and execution choices. These resources provide valuable infrastructure and task-level diagnostics, whereas a complete adaptive search process additionally requires controlling the sequence of proposals, public feedback, and final portfolio evaluation.

Evaluating an adaptive quantitative research agent requires controlling the process that produced its final output. Repeated access to noisy backtests creates opportunities for multiple testing and selection bias \citep{Bailey2017Backtest}. \BenchmarkName\ addresses this requirement through frozen data releases, date-scoped execution, registered resource budgets, bounded research feedback, explicit financial conventions, and sealed assessment of one committed strategy. Its Arena platform preserves these conditions across model interactions, quantitative execution, and recoverable failures. The resulting protocol complements financial research libraries and task benchmarks with an operational account of complete LLM research runs.
