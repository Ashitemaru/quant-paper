\subsection{Task Formulation}
\label{sec:task}

A \emph{suite} fixes everything a research run depends on:
\begin{equation}
    q = (\mathcal{D},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{E},\mathbf{B},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}),
    \label{eq:suite}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
    \item $\mathcal{D}$ is an immutable data release;
    \item $\mathcal{U}$ is the stock universe;
    \item $\mathcal{W}$ are the chronological research and final windows;
    \item $\mathcal{E}$ is the execution environment;
    \item $\mathbf{B}$ is the research budget;
    \item $\mathcal{F}$ is the feedback policy;
    \item $\mathcal{V}$ is the financial evaluator and scoring rule.
\end{itemize}
Any change to $q$ defines a new suite. Scores are compared only within a suite.

A \emph{run} registers a model, a track, and a harness under $q$. The run proceeds in three steps.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Research.} The harness alternates model calls and research evaluations on the in-sample window, within budget $\mathbf{B}$, receiving only the feedback permitted by $\mathcal{F}$.
    \item \textbf{Commitment.} The run commits exactly one strategy. Its source code, runtime, and declared features are frozen.
    \item \textbf{Sealed assessment.} The platform evaluates the frozen strategy once on the out-of-sample window. Nothing from this window is returned to the model, and the run cannot revise its strategy afterwards.
\end{enumerate}
The committed strategy, not the best intermediate candidate, is what the leaderboard scores. This separates research skill from the luck of reporting a favorable trial.

A strategy is a Python program. On each trading day it receives authorized market observations up to that day and returns a score for every stock in the universe. It may also declare up to 32 named features that it combines into its score.

\subsection{Market Environment}
\label{sec:data}

The first public suite covers the Shenzhen A-share market:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Universe:} a static universe of 3,042 Shenzhen-listed stocks. Missing observations and delisted stocks are retained rather than filtered.
    \item \textbf{Data:} raw daily and Level-2 market data, served through a date-scoped data interface. Each day's strategy process can read only data available for that day. Access is enforced by the execution service, not by the strategy.
    \item \textbf{Windows:} 2022--2023 is the in-sample research window, and 2024 is the sealed out-of-sample window. The research period was a falling market. 2024 contains a sharp market drawdown in February followed by a strong rally in the autumn, so the final year doubles as a stress test.
    \item \textbf{Budget:} each run has 500,000 model tokens and at most five research evaluations.
\end{itemize}

Each trading day runs in a fresh, isolated low-privilege process. State can cross days only through declared artifacts. Model credentials and market-data permissions are held by trusted platform services. Raw market matrices and evaluation internals never enter the model conversation. Appendix~\ref{app:platform} describes the execution platform.

\subsection{Tracks and Harnesses}
\label{sec:tracks}

The research loop is run by a \emph{harness}: a program that calls the model, submits strategies for evaluation, and decides when to stop. Participants enter in one of two ways.

\paragraph{Platform harnesses.} The platform provides two standard harnesses, which define the two tracks studied in this paper.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{One-shot.} The model reads the task and the platform documentation and submits one complete strategy. The strategy is frozen without any research-performance feedback. Invalid output may be repaired, but the model never sees how its strategy performs.
    \item \textbf{Iterative research.} The model submits a strategy and receives in-sample research feedback: return, signal, and coverage statistics, plus execution errors. It may then revise, up to five evaluations and the token budget. It ends by selecting one completed candidate; if the budget runs out, the last eligible candidate is frozen.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Own harness.} Participants may submit their own agent harness through the Arena SDK. It runs under the same data, budget, and evaluation contract. Such entries form a separate \emph{own-agent} track.

Model calls are routed through the platform gateway, which records token usage. Only calls to verified official model endpoints count toward the official tracks. Entries using unverified self-hosted endpoints are ranked separately as \emph{self-hosted}.

\subsection{Financial Evaluation}
\label{sec:financial}

Each day, the strategy's scores select the 75 highest-scoring stocks, with ties at the boundary retained, as an equal-weight long portfolio held for one trading day. Top-200 and Top-400 portfolios are reported as diagnostics.

The portfolio is booked on a \emph{cash account}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
    \item Trades respect available cash and 100-share lots.
    \item A stock at its upper price limit cannot be bought. Unfilled purchases stay in cash and are not replaced by other stocks.
    \item A suspended stock, or one at its lower price limit, cannot be sold. Its sale is deferred to the first day it can be sold.
    \item Slippage is computed from order-book limits and market impact rather than set to a fixed value.
    \item A day without a valid signal opens no position, and its return is zero.
    \item Remaining positions are liquidated at the end of each window.
\end{itemize}
The account's daily net asset value (NAV) is compounded over the window.

From each window we compute the following metrics, using 243 trading days per year:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Account metrics:}
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
        \item annualized return (ARR);
        \item excess ARR $r$ over a full-market equal-weight reference on the same days (the reference is gross of costs);
        \item long-only Sharpe ratio $\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{L}}$;
        \item maximum drawdown.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Signal metrics:}
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
        \item mean daily cross-sectional RankIC between scores and next-period returns;
        \item annualized RankICIR $A$ (mean over standard deviation of daily RankIC), which measures signal stability;
        \item long--short Sharpe $\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{LS}}$ of the top-minus-bottom decile;
        \item turnover of the Top-75 portfolio.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Resource and diversity:}
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
        \item tokens used $U$;
        \item number $K$ of independent feature clusters the strategy actually uses.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
To compute $K$, we take the declared features that pass a quality screen, group correlated ones into clusters, and test each cluster's contribution by regressing the strategy's score ranks on the feature ranks. Copying a feature therefore does not count twice (Appendix~\ref{app:diversity}).

\subsection{Scoring}
\label{sec:scoring}

Each window receives a score from 0 to 100. Let $x^+=\max(0,x)$. Every metric is mapped to $[0,1]$ with diminishing returns:
\begin{gather}
 s_{\mathrm{IC}}=\sqrt{\mathrm{RankIC}^+},\quad
 s_{\mathrm{ST}}=\frac{A^+}{1+A^+},\quad
 s_{\mathrm{L}}=\frac{\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{L}}^+}{1+\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{L}}^+},\quad
 s_{\mathrm{LS}}=\frac{\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{LS}}^+}{1+\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{LS}}^+},\nonumber\\
 s_{\mathrm{TO}}=\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{Turnover}},\qquad
 s_{\mathrm{R}}=\frac{r^+}{0.3+r^+}.
 \label{eq:terms}
\end{gather}
For example, $s_{\mathrm{R}}=0.5$ at 30\% annualized excess return.

\paragraph{Quality.} Signal quality is the weighted geometric mean of the five signal terms. Quality adds the excess-return term:
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\mathrm{sig}}=\left(s_{\mathrm{IC}}\,s_{\mathrm{ST}}\,s_{\mathrm{L}}\,s_{\mathrm{LS}}\right)^{0.225}s_{\mathrm{TO}}^{0.1},\qquad
 Q=0.7\,Q_{\mathrm{sig}}+0.3\,s_{\mathrm{R}}.
 \label{eq:quality}
\end{equation}
The geometric mean means a strategy must be good on every signal dimension. The excess-return term rewards beating the market rather than riding it: a strategy that simply follows the market earns $s_{\mathrm{R}}=0$.

\paragraph{Efficiency and diversity.} With token budget $B=500{,}000$,
\begin{equation}
 E=Q\left(1-\frac{U}{B}\right),\qquad
 D=Q\,\frac{\min(K,32)}{32}.
 \label{eq:ed}
\end{equation}
Both are scaled by $Q$, so spending no tokens or declaring many features earns nothing without a good strategy.

\paragraph{Window and total scores.} The in-sample window score is
\begin{equation}
 S_{\mathrm{in}}=
 \begin{cases}
 75\,Q+15\,E+10\,D & \text{one-shot and iterative research},\\
 80\,Q+20\,D & \text{own-agent and self-hosted},
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
where efficiency is dropped for harnesses whose token use the platform cannot attribute.

The out-of-sample score $S_{\mathrm{out}}$ uses the same metrics on the sealed 2024 window. Its exact combination is not published and may change between versions, so that the sealed year cannot be optimized against a known formula. The total is
\begin{equation}
 S=0.3\,S_{\mathrm{in}}+0.7\,S_{\mathrm{out}}.
 \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Leaderboard rules.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
    \item Each submission runs once. Its result is published automatically and cannot be withdrawn by the participant.
    \item Each account has a daily submission limit, which prevents repeated sampling of the sealed year.
    \item A run whose token accounting is incomplete, or that fails its research evaluation, receives no total score. Its attempt is still counted.
\end{itemize}
