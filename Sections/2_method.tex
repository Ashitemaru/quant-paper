\section{\BenchmarkName: Task and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\BenchmarkName\ evaluates an end-to-end quantitative research workflow, from the initial model interaction to the sealed assessment of a frozen strategy. The benchmark defines the evaluation protocol, whereas the Arena platform executes it. This decoupling permits diverse research procedures while preserving the strict information barriers and measurement standards applied to their outputs.

\subsection{Task, Participant, and Evaluation Unit}
\label{sec:arena_task}

We define a benchmark suite as
\begin{equation}
    q = (\mathcal{D},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{E},\mathbf{B},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}),
    \label{eq:arena_task}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}$ identifies immutable data releases, $\mathcal{U}$ the authorized instrument population, $\mathcal{W}$ the chronological windows and warmup rules, $\mathcal{P}$ the input profile, $\mathcal{E}$ the execution environment, $\mathbf{B}$ the resource budgets, $\mathcal{F}$ the feedback policy, and $\mathcal{V}$ the financial and scoring evaluator. All components are frozen before a matched comparison. A change to this contract defines a new benchmark setting.

A participant $a=(M,H)$ couples a registered language model $M$ with a research harness $H$, which governs prompt construction, candidate generation, feedback interpretation, stopping criteria, and final selection. A replicate executes this participant under $q$ with a registered seed identifier and an independent budget. An episode comprises the prescribed replicates, whose complete records constitute the unit of comparison. Harness modifications, including alternative prior memories or candidate-selection policies, define participant variants within the same suite.

The platform supports three input profiles: authorized raw market data, fifteen-second order-book observations, and anonymous numerical features. These profiles exhibit distinct informational granularities and preprocessing demands, and are evaluated under separate conditions. Strategies generate cross-sectional stock scores across authorized signal dates and may explicitly declare constituent features. When utilized, an optional training phase operates over an earlier registered interval, after which the resulting model artifact is frozen and reused across both research and final evaluation. Both participant-defined scoring routines and trusted linear combinations of declared features are supported, with the latter providing directly verifiable evidence of feature utilization for diversity assessment.

\subsection{Research Interaction and Resource Constraints}
\label{sec:arena_interaction}

The evaluation harness alternates between model interactions and admitted research evaluations. Each completed evaluation returns strictly the projection permitted by $\mathcal{F}$, including selected metrics, validity and coverage information, execution summaries, and remaining resources. Metric precision and available diagnostics are fixed by the suite, whereas private signal matrices, quantitative execution logs, fitted artifacts, and trading records are withheld from the research feedback channel.

The current budget profile distinguishes model tokens, admitted research evaluations, and execution CPU usage. Let $u_j$ be the reported token usage of model operation $j$, $n_{\mathrm{adm}}$ the number of admitted research evaluations, and $c_\ell$ the measured CPU usage of execution $\ell$. The corresponding usage vector is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{b} = \left(\sum_j u_j,\ n_{\mathrm{adm}},\ \sum_\ell c_\ell\right),
    \qquad \mathbf{b} \preceq \mathbf{B}
    \quad\text{for budget-eligible runs}.
    \label{eq:budgets}
\end{equation}
These sums apply when the required usage records are known; unresolved usage retains an explicit unavailable state. Admission also accounts for outstanding reservations and protects CPU capacity for final evaluation. Reservations are released or settled against observations and do not become measured consumption by definition. CPU accounting covers registered executions rather than all shared-service overhead. Token ceilings do not imply equal monetary expenditure or equal effective computation across models.

A malformed model response incurs the consumption of its observed tokens. A strategy rejected prior to evaluation admission does not deplete the admitted evaluation quota; however, an accepted evaluation retains its charge even if execution subsequently fails. Data requests and transmitted bytes remain metered under the prevailing service-limit profile, subject to access and concurrency controls, while historical profiles preserve their respective cumulative resource definitions.

Three participation modes define distinct experimental conditions. In \emph{One-shot} mode, the model produces a single strategy that is immediately frozen, without research feedback, automated repair, or additional training; platform documentation may be queried on demand, with each retrieval counted as a standard model call. The \emph{Vanilla} mode employs an interactive model--feedback dialogue: the process concludes once the model outputs a completed candidate, with the most recent candidate evaluated with feedback serving as the designated fallback if the budget is exhausted. Finally, \emph{External harnesses} execute custom search and selection rules through the same authorized services, with their external knowledge, memory initialization, repair policy, and selection rule formalized as part of the participant specification.

\subsection{Temporal Access and Final Commitment}
\label{sec:arena_contract}

The training, research, and final accounting windows are sequenced chronologically. An authorized warmup phase supplies historical context without extending the accounting interval. For a signal date $d$, the executable strategy receives exclusively those observations within the authorized data release, instrument and field scope, and availability interval terminating at $\kappa(d)$. The boundary $\kappa(d)$ is registered within the suite and remains undisclosed in the public contract. Data permissions are enforced directly by the execution service rather than inferred from dates provided by participant code.

Following the research phase, each replicate commits a single strategy. Its source code, runtime environment, fitted artifacts (when present), and declared feature configuration are frozen prior to sealed assessment. The harness governs selection, while the platform validates and locks the commitment prior to executing the final evaluator. Outcomes from the final window are strictly isolated from candidate revision within that replicate. Subsequent evaluation under an alternative contract generates a separate record, thereby preserving the original result.

The daily protocol employs registered static Shenzhen A-share factor columns. Missing observations are preserved, and no daily index-constituent mask is applied. Population construction, delisted securities, and data-publication histories must therefore be disclosed alongside the evaluation suite. While runtime authorization regulates access during execution, historical knowledge embedded in model pretraining or participant priors introduces an orthogonal source of potential contamination. Repeated public leaderboard access likewise necessitates a campaign-level disclosure policy, as detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:boundary}.

\subsection{Daily Financial Evaluation}
\label{sec:arena_returns}

We index consecutive trading sessions by $t$. The selection scores $s_{i,t-1}$ are constructed exclusively from information authorized through session $t-1$. The designated cohort is acquired on trading day $t$ and liquidated on day $t+1$ via algorithmic execution. Rather than being modeled as a static parameter, slippage is dynamically evaluated using order-book constraints and market impact. Securities at their upper price limit are barred from entry, whereas halted or limit-down positions are divested once market accessibility resumes, with no compensatory replacement assets selected. An authorized predecessor signal may validate the initial entry date; however, absent signals are not retroactively reconstructed using subsequent observations, and missing source data yield no actionable signal.

\paragraph{Selection.}
Let $\mathcal{I}_t$ contain the registered instruments with finite scores for entry $t$, ranked in descending order. For a valid signal row, let $s_{[k],t-1}$ be its $k$-th largest score and define
\begin{equation}
    k_t=\min(K,|\mathcal{I}_t|),\qquad
    \mathcal{S}^{K}_t=\{i\in\mathcal{I}_t:s_{i,t-1}\ge s_{[k_t],t-1}\}.
    \label{eq:topk}
\end{equation}
This definition includes every boundary tie, so the selected count may exceed $K$. Selection precedes the observation of forward prices and does not replace securities whose returns later prove unavailable. The primary basket uses $K=75$; $K\in\{200,400\}$ are retained as diagnostics.

\paragraph{Research returns.}
Let $y_{i,t}$ be the realized raw-price return of stock $i$ over the cohort's holding period and $\mathcal{A}^{K}_t\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{K}_t$ the selected stocks with a valid return. The cohort research return $r^K_t$ is the equal-weight mean of $y_{i,t}$ over $\mathcal{A}^K_t$, net of trading costs. Cohorts overlap and returns accumulate additively:
\begin{align}
    R^K &= \sum_{t\in\mathcal{J}_K}r^K_t,
    &\operatorname{ARR}^{K} &= \frac{243}{|\mathcal{J}_K|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{J}_K}r^K_t,\label{eq:research_arr}\\
    \Delta\operatorname{ARR}^{K} &= \frac{243}{|\mathcal{J}^{\cap}_K|}
       \sum_{t\in\mathcal{J}^{\cap}_K}(r^K_t-r^{\mathrm{mkt}}_t),
       \label{eq:excess_arr}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{J}_K$ are entry sessions with an available return, $r^{\mathrm{mkt}}_t$ is the full-market Shenzhen A-share reference under the same convention, and $\mathcal{J}^{\cap}_K$ are paired sessions. An empty observable set yields an unavailable return rather than zero. Signed additive drawdown is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{DD}^{K}
    =\min_{0\le j\le |\mathcal{J}_K|}
       \left(P^K_j-\max_{0\le h\le j}P^K_h\right)\le0,
    \label{eq:drawdown}
\end{equation}
with $P^K_j$ the cumulative sum through the $j$-th available cohort. The long-only Sharpe ratio is $\mathrm{SR}_L=\sqrt{243}\,\overline{r^{75}}/\operatorname{sd}(r^{75})$. The long--short Sharpe ratio $\mathrm{SR}_{LS}$ applies the same annualization to the daily raw return of the top score decile minus the bottom decile; it measures the signal's discriminative power, not a tradable return. Because adjacent cohorts overlap, both ratios are descriptive. A funded cash account is simulated and reported separately from research returns.

\subsection{Scoring}
\label{sec:scoring}
\label{sec:arena_scoring}
% DRAFT (Claude): formulas are final; prose to be rewritten by Gemini.

On each valid entry date, RankIC is the cross-sectional rank correlation between $s_{i,t-1}$ and $y_{i,t}$; $\overline{\mathrm{RankIC}}$ is its daily mean and RankICIR the mean divided by the sample standard deviation. Turnover $\mathrm{TO}\in[0,1]$ is the mean daily fraction of the Top75 basket that is replaced. With $x^+=\max(0,x)$, the in-sample (2022--2023) quality score is the weighted geometric mean
\begin{equation}
    Q=\Big(\sqrt{\overline{\mathrm{RankIC}}^{+}}\Big)^{w_1}
      \Big(\tfrac{A^+}{1+A^+}\Big)^{w_2}
      \Big(\tfrac{\mathrm{SR}_L^+}{1+\mathrm{SR}_L^+}\Big)^{w_3}
      \Big(\tfrac{\mathrm{SR}_{LS}^+}{1+\mathrm{SR}_{LS}^+}\Big)^{w_4}
      \Big(\tfrac{1}{1+\mathrm{TO}}\Big)^{w_5},
    \qquad A=\mathrm{RankICIR}\cdot\sqrt{243},
    \label{eq:quality}
\end{equation}
with $(w_1,\dots,w_5)=(0.225,0.225,0.225,0.225,0.1)$. Each term lies in $[0,1]$ with diminishing returns. Turnover enters as $1/(1+\mathrm{TO})$ so that a fully rotating basket halves this term instead of zeroing $Q$. Efficiency and diversity are
\begin{equation}
    E=Q\left(1-\frac{T}{B}\right),\qquad
    D=Q\,\frac{\min(K,32)}{32},
    \label{eq:efficiency}
\end{equation}
where $T$ is the provider-reported token usage, $B=500{,}000$ the token budget, and $K$ the number of qualified, used, and mutually independent feature clusters (Appendix~\ref{app:diversity}). The window score is
\begin{equation}
    S=\begin{cases}70Q+20E+10D & \text{one-shot and vanilla tracks},\\ 80Q+20D & \text{system and custom tracks,}\end{cases}
    \label{eq:overall}
\end{equation}
on a 0--100 scale, and the leaderboard total is
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Total}=0.3\,S_{\mathrm{in}}+0.7\,S_{\mathrm{out}}.
    \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
The in-sample formula, its parameters, and all component scores are published. The out-of-sample (2024) score $S_{\mathrm{out}}$ takes into account RankIC, IC stability, long-only and long--short Sharpe ratios, turnover, factor diversity, and token usage; its combination is not disclosed and may be revised independently. Raw metrics of both windows are published.

Unavailable metrics preserve their respective reason codes and sample counts and are never replaced by zero. Failed runs are retained across all denominators; averages evaluated strictly over completed runs are explicitly designated as conditional statistics.
