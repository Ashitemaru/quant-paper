\section{\BenchmarkName: Task and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\BenchmarkName\ is a released benchmark contract for complete quantitative research runs, from the first model interaction to a frozen strategy's sealed assessment. The benchmark fixes the information, resource, interaction, financial, and scoring rules; the Arena platform executes and records them.

\subsection{Task and Evaluation Unit}
\label{sec:arena_task}

We define a benchmark suite as
\begin{equation}
    q = (\mathcal{D},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{E},\mathbf{B},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}),
    \label{eq:arena_task}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}$ identifies immutable data releases, $\mathcal{U}$ the authorized instrument population, $\mathcal{W}$ the chronological windows and warmup rules, $\mathcal{P}$ the input profile, $\mathcal{E}$ the execution environment, $\mathbf{B}$ the resource budgets, $\mathcal{F}$ the feedback policy, and $\mathcal{V}$ the financial and scoring evaluator. All components are frozen before a matched comparison. A change to this contract defines a new benchmark setting.

A participant combines a registered model with the research procedure that controls prompts, permitted feedback, repairs, stopping, and final selection. In this campaign, the procedure is specified by the interaction track and its implementation. Each replicate executes that participant under $q$ with its own resource allowance and commits one final strategy, or ends without a completed assessment. An episode contains the prescribed replicates and retains all their outcomes. The reported campaign requires one replicate per episode; repeated episodes under the same model--track condition provide the observations summarized in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

The run specification includes model identity, track implementation, strategy source, seed identifier, and suite. Appendix~\ref{app:registry} distinguishes the retained campaign records from provenance artifacts still needed for replication. Changes to the research procedure or suite define distinct comparison conditions. Execution status, metric availability, and score eligibility are reported separately.

The platform supports three input profiles: authorized raw market data, fifteen-second order-book observations, and anonymous numerical features. They expose different information and preprocessing requirements and are evaluated as distinct conditions. Strategies return cross-sectional stock scores for authorized signal dates and may declare constituent features. Optional training uses an earlier registered interval; the resulting model artifact is frozen and reused in research and final evaluation. The reported campaign uses registered Python scoring programs. The platform also supports trusted linear combinations in separately configured suites. Feature-use evidence for the reported Python programs is obtained through the score-rank regression described in Appendix~\ref{app:diversity}.

\subsection{Research Interaction and Resource Constraints}
\label{sec:arena_interaction}

The model interaction service alternates model calls and admitted research evaluations. Each completed evaluation returns the projection permitted by $\mathcal{F}$, including selected metrics, validity and coverage information, execution summaries, and remaining resources. Metric precision and available diagnostics are fixed by the suite. Private signal matrices, quantitative execution logs, fitted artifacts, and trading records are withheld from the research feedback channel.

The current budget profile distinguishes model tokens, charged research evaluations, and execution CPU usage. Let $u_j$ be the reported token usage of model operation $j$, $n_{\mathrm{chg}}$ the number of charged research evaluations, and $c_\ell$ the measured CPU usage of execution $\ell$. The corresponding usage vector is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{b} = \left(\sum_j u_j,\ n_{\mathrm{chg}},\ \sum_\ell c_\ell\right),
    \qquad \mathbf{b} \preceq \mathbf{B}
    \quad\text{for budget-eligible benchmark runs}.
    \label{eq:budgets}
\end{equation}
These sums apply when the required usage records are known; unresolved usage retains an explicit unavailable state. Admission also accounts for outstanding reservations and protects CPU capacity for final evaluation. Reservations are released or settled against observations and do not become measured consumption by definition. CPU accounting covers registered executions rather than all shared-service overhead. Token ceilings do not imply equal monetary expenditure or equal effective computation across models.

A malformed model reply consumes its observed tokens. A strategy rejected before evaluation admission does not consume an evaluation; an accepted evaluation retains its charge when the strategy fails. Platform faults and cancellation do not become charged research evaluations. Data requests and bytes remain metered under the current service-limit profile, with access and concurrency controls; historical profiles retain their own cumulative resource definitions.

The released benchmark exposes two interaction tracks in the reported campaign. \emph{One-shot} submits one complete strategy and freezes it without research-performance feedback or candidate search. Under the campaign's document-reading interaction, the model can first request platform documentation; these requests consume model tokens and calls but no research evaluation. Document requests and validity repairs can require multiple provider calls. Throughout this paper, one-shot denotes strategy generation without research-performance feedback. \emph{Vanilla} uses a model--feedback conversation: the model may finish with a completed candidate, and the last eligible completed trial is the fallback when the budget ends. The charging rules above apply to both tracks; a blocked evaluation retains its reservation until resolved.

\subsection{Temporal Access and Final Commitment}
\label{sec:arena_contract}

Training, research, and final accounting windows are registered chronologically. Authorized warmup supplies historical context without extending the accounting interval. The executable strategy receives observations only within its authorized data release, instrument and field scope, and temporal access policy. Data permissions are enforced by the execution service. Exact data-availability cutoffs and execution times are retained privately by the evaluator.

After research, each replicate commits one strategy. Its source, runtime, fitted artifact when present, and declared feature configuration are fixed before sealed assessment. The registered interaction track controls the selection; the platform validates and freezes the commitment and runs the final evaluator. Final-window outcomes are excluded from candidate revision within that replicate. Further evaluation under another contract produces a separate record and preserves the original result.

The daily protocol uses registered static Shenzhen A-share factor columns. Missing observations are retained, and no daily index-constituent mask is imposed. Population construction, delisted securities, and data-publication history must therefore be disclosed alongside the suite. Runtime authorization controls access during the experiment; historical knowledge in model pretraining or prior resources remains a separate source of possible contamination. Repeated public leaderboard access also requires a campaign-level disclosure policy, as discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:boundary}.

\subsection{Financial Evaluation and Versioning}
\label{sec:arena_returns}

Each strategy assigns scores to the authorized stock universe. The primary portfolio selects the highest-scoring 75 stocks, retaining all boundary ties; Top200 and Top400 are diagnostic portfolios. Invalid signals and missing observations are recorded explicitly. The public protocol describes portfolio construction and evaluation dimensions without disclosing exact trade times, data-availability cutoffs, or price-alignment rules.

\paragraph{Cash-account protocol.}
The revised protocol uses a cash account with daily net asset values. Accounting respects available cash, 100-share lots, and transaction costs. Price limits and suspensions constrain execution: unavailable purchases are not replaced by other stocks, and blocked sales may be deferred. A session without a valid signal opens no new position; the account record retains cash and any outstanding holdings. Return-based evaluation uses this account series. Exact execution scheduling and cost parameters are not specified here.

Annualized return summarizes compounded account performance using 243 trading sessions per year. Long-only Sharpe measures absolute daily return relative to its variability, and maximum drawdown describes the largest decline from a prior account-value peak. A market equal-weight reference provides context for excess return. These financial statistics accompany signal quality and coverage; they do not by themselves establish live trading capacity.

\paragraph{Result provenance.}
The archive retains historical scorecards on an additive research basis and supplies offline re-evaluation on the cash-account basis. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} uses the latter for its main financial comparisons, independently checking account statistics against saved net asset values. Historical values remain separately labelled in the appendix. Offline score outputs are identified by their revision and are not presented as a confirmed online deployment.

\subsection{Public Research Scoring and Private Final Assessment}
\label{sec:arena_scoring}

The revised protocol considers predictive ordering, signal stability, long-only and long--short performance, realized account return, turnover, resource efficiency, and diversity of qualified features. Coverage and missingness accompany these measurements. The research-window scoring rule is public. The final evaluator considers the same categories, but its transformations, weights, aggregation, and contribution to the published total are not disclosed. Final scoring is versioned; a later campaign may adopt a different private combination. The public formula specifies research feedback only and cannot be used to reconstruct final scores.

\paragraph{Public research-window formula.}
The following equations apply only to the revised \emph{in-sample} protocol. Let $x^+=\max(0,x)$, let $A$ be annualized RankICIR, and let $\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{L}}$ and $\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{LS}}$ denote long-only and long--short Sharpe. RankIC measures cross-sectional rank agreement with subsequent returns, averaged across available dates; $A$ describes its temporal stability. Turnover measures replacement in the selected portfolio. Define
\begin{align}
 z_{\mathrm{IC}}&=\sqrt{\overline{\operatorname{RankIC}}^+}, &
 z_{\mathrm{ST}}&=\frac{A^+}{1+A^+},\\
 z_{\mathrm{L}}&=\frac{\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{L}}^+}{1+\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{L}}^+}, &
 z_{\mathrm{LS}}&=\frac{\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{LS}}^+}{1+\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{LS}}^+},\\
 z_{\mathrm{TO}}&=\frac{1}{1+\operatorname{Turnover}}, &
 z_{\mathrm{R}}&=\frac{r_{\mathrm{IS}}^+}{0.3+r_{\mathrm{IS}}^+}.
\end{align}
Here $r_{\mathrm{IS}}$ is cash-account ARR minus the aligned gross equal-weight market reference ARR on the research window. Long-only Sharpe also uses the cash-account basis. The reference is gross of costs and is not a matched tradable baseline. The research quality terms are
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\mathrm{IS}}=(z_{\mathrm{IC}}z_{\mathrm{ST}}z_{\mathrm{L}}z_{\mathrm{LS}})^{0.225}z_{\mathrm{TO}}^{0.1},\qquad
 Q'_{\mathrm{IS}}=0.7Q_{\mathrm{IS}}+0.3z_{\mathrm{R}}.
 \label{eq:quality}
\end{equation}
The additive excess-return term permits a positive research score even when a component of the geometric signal term is zero. With recorded token usage $U_{\mathrm{tok}}$, token allowance $B_{\mathrm{tok}}=500{,}000$, and $K_{\mathrm{IS}}$ qualified feature groups that contribute to the strategy,
\begin{equation}
 E_{\mathrm{IS}}=Q'_{\mathrm{IS}}\left(1-\frac{U_{\mathrm{tok}}}{B_{\mathrm{tok}}}\right),\qquad
 D_{\mathrm{IS}}=Q'_{\mathrm{IS}}\frac{\min(K_{\mathrm{IS}},32)}{32}.
 \label{eq:efficiency}
\end{equation}
For budget-eligible one-shot and vanilla runs, the revised research-window score is
\begin{equation}
 S_{\mathrm{IS}}=75Q'_{\mathrm{IS}}+15E_{\mathrm{IS}}+10D_{\mathrm{IS}}.
 \label{eq:research_score}
\end{equation}
These public research coefficients are version-specific. They are not used to reconstruct the historical totals in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, and they do not specify the final-window computation.

\paragraph{Availability and disclosure.}
Execution completion, financial availability, and score availability are distinct. Missing metrics are reported without imputation. Incomplete token accounting or an unavailable required window assessment prevents publication of a total score, while available financial results remain reportable. For a strategy frozen without a research trial, the platform can assess the research window after commitment; that assessment is not feedback to the model. Detailed final-scoring traces remain private. Public records identify the evaluator version and reported outcomes, and independent recomputation of the private final score requires organizer access.
