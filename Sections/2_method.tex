\section{\BenchmarkName: Task and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\BenchmarkName\ records research episodes from the first model interaction through final assessment or an unsuccessful terminal state. This section defines the comparison unit, permitted interaction, and measurement rules. The Arena mechanisms that enforce them appear in Section~\ref{sec:arena}.

\subsection{Task, Participant, and Evaluation Unit}
\label{sec:arena_task}

We define a benchmark suite as
\begin{equation}
    q = (\mathcal{D},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{E},\mathbf{B},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}),
    \label{eq:arena_task}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}$ identifies immutable data releases, $\mathcal{U}$ the authorized instrument population, $\mathcal{W}$ the chronological windows and warmup rules, $\mathcal{P}$ the input profile, $\mathcal{E}$ the execution environment, $\mathbf{B}$ the resource budgets, $\mathcal{F}$ the feedback policy, and $\mathcal{V}$ the financial and scoring evaluator. All components are frozen before a matched comparison. A change to this contract defines a new benchmark setting.

A participant $a=(M,H)$ combines a registered language model $M$ with a research harness $H$. The harness determines prompt construction, candidate generation, feedback interpretation, stopping, and final selection. A replicate executes this participant under $q$ with a registered seed identifier and its own budget. An episode contains the prescribed replicates; their complete records constitute the unit of comparison. Harness changes, including different prior memories or candidate-selection policies, define participant variants under the same suite.

Table~\ref{tab:terminology} fixes the terms used throughout the paper. Execution status, metric availability, and ranking eligibility describe different properties: a completed assessment can yield an unavailable metric, and an available financial result can remain outside a particular ranking. Recording every prescribed replicate does not imply that every replicate succeeded.

The platform supports three input profiles: authorized raw market data, fifteen-second order-book observations, and anonymous numerical features. They expose different information and preprocessing requirements and are evaluated as distinct conditions. Strategies return cross-sectional stock scores for authorized signal dates and may declare constituent features. Optional training uses an earlier registered interval; the resulting model artifact is frozen and reused in research and final evaluation. Participants may submit executable scoring programs or weighted combinations of declared features. The latter expose feature use directly to the evaluator.

\subsection{Research Interaction and Resource Constraints}
\label{sec:arena_interaction}

The harness alternates between model interactions and admitted research evaluations. Each completed evaluation returns the projection permitted by $\mathcal{F}$, including selected metrics, validity and coverage information, execution summaries, and remaining resources. Metric precision and available diagnostics are fixed by the suite. Private signal matrices, quantitative execution logs, fitted artifacts, and trading records are withheld from the research feedback channel.

The current budget profile distinguishes model tokens, admitted research evaluations, and execution CPU usage. Let $u_j$ be the reported token usage of model operation $j$, $n_{\mathrm{adm}}$ the number of admitted research evaluations, and $c_\ell$ the measured CPU usage of execution $\ell$. The corresponding usage vector is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{obs}} = \left(\sum_j u_j,\ n_{\mathrm{adm}},\ \sum_\ell c_\ell\right).
    \label{eq:budgets}
\end{equation}
These sums apply when the required usage records are known; unresolved usage retains an explicit unavailable state. Budget eligibility checks the separately recorded charged vector, $\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{chg}}\preceq\mathbf{B}$. Known measurements settle their charges to the observed values. In the current implementation, unknown token usage is charged at the reserved amount and unknown CPU usage is charged at zero; neither rule resolves the missing measurement. Admission also accounts for outstanding reservations and protects CPU capacity for final evaluation. CPU accounting covers registered executions rather than all shared-service overhead. Token ceilings do not imply equal monetary expenditure or equal effective computation across models.

A malformed model reply consumes its observed tokens. A strategy rejected before evaluation admission does not consume an admitted evaluation; an accepted evaluation retains its evaluation charge if execution subsequently fails. Data requests and bytes remain metered under the current service-limit profile, with access and concurrency controls; historical profiles retain their own cumulative resource definitions.

Three participation modes establish different research conditions. \emph{One-shot} permits one model operation and directly freezes a valid strategy, without research feedback, model-assisted repair, or additional training. \emph{Vanilla} uses a model--feedback conversation: the model may finish with a completed candidate, and the last completed candidate with feedback is the declared fallback when the budget ends. \emph{External harnesses} implement their own search and selection rules through the same authorized services. Their external knowledge, memory initialization, repair policy, and selection rule form part of the participant specification.

\subsection{Temporal Access and Final Commitment}
\label{sec:arena_contract}

Training, research, and final accounting windows are registered chronologically. Authorized warmup supplies historical context without extending the accounting interval. For a signal date $d$, the executable strategy receives only observations in the authorized data release, instrument and field scope, and availability interval ending at $\kappa(d)$. In the current daily protocol, $\kappa(d)$ is the end of that trading day in exchange-local time, including its closing information. Data permissions are enforced by the execution service rather than inferred from dates supplied by participant code.

After research, each replicate commits one strategy. Its source, runtime, fitted artifact when present, and declared feature configuration are fixed before sealed assessment. The harness controls the selection; the platform validates and freezes the commitment and runs the final evaluator. Final-window outcomes are excluded from candidate revision within that replicate. Further evaluation under another contract produces a separate record and preserves the original result.

The daily protocol uses registered static Shenzhen A-share factor columns. Missing observations are retained, and no daily index-constituent mask is imposed. Population construction, delisted securities, and data-publication history must therefore be disclosed alongside the suite. Runtime authorization controls access during the experiment; historical knowledge in model pretraining or participant priors remains a separate source of possible contamination. Repeated public leaderboard access also requires a campaign-level disclosure policy, as discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:boundary}.

\subsection{Daily Financial Evaluation}
\label{sec:arena_returns}

Index consecutive trading sessions by $t$. Scores $s_{i,t-1}$ are formed using information authorized through session $t-1$; a selected cohort enters at session $t$'s opening price and is scheduled to exit at session $t+1$'s closing price. Both entry and exit belong to the accounting window. An authorized predecessor signal can support the first entry day; an absent predecessor is not reconstructed from later data. The last accounting session introduces no new cohort.

\paragraph{Selection and observable labels.}
Let $\mathcal{I}_t$ contain the registered instruments with finite scores for entry $t$, with score direction fixed so that larger values rank first. For a valid signal row, let $s_{[k],t-1}$ be its $k$-th largest score and define
\begin{equation}
    k_t=\min(K,|\mathcal{I}_t|),\qquad
    \mathcal{S}^{K}_t=\{i\in\mathcal{I}_t:s_{i,t-1}\ge s_{[k_t],t-1}\}.
    \label{eq:topk}
\end{equation}
This definition includes every boundary tie, so the selected count may exceed $K$. Empty or invalid signal rows have no evaluable cohort. The current validity check requires observed entry-universe scores with at least two distinct finite values. Selection precedes forward-price observation and does not replace securities whose labels later prove unavailable.

For raw opening and closing prices $O$ and $C$, the forward label is
\begin{equation}
    y_{i,t}=\frac{C_{i,t+1}}{O_{i,t}}-1,\qquad
    \mathcal{L}_t=\{i:\text{the registered label-validity rules are satisfied}\}.
    \label{eq:label}
\end{equation}
Label validity requires the relevant daily records, non-suspended observations, and finite positive prices; the implementation checks both open and close fields of each relevant bar. Let $\mathcal{A}^{K}_t=\mathcal{S}^{K}_t\cap\mathcal{L}_t$. For a valid nonempty observable cohort, the research return and label coverage are
\begin{equation}
    r^{K}_t=\frac{\eta}{|\mathcal{A}^{K}_t|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{A}^{K}_t}y_{i,t}-c,
    \qquad
    \gamma^{K}_t=\frac{|\mathcal{A}^{K}_t|}{|\mathcal{S}^{K}_t|},
    \quad \eta=\tfrac12,\quad c=0.0011.
    \label{eq:cohort_return}
\end{equation}
An empty observable set yields an unavailable return. Partially observed cohorts retain their observed-only mean and coverage. The fixed cost is deducted once after the half-exposure scaling. The primary basket uses $K=75$, with $K\in\{200,400\}$ retained for diagnostics.

\paragraph{Aggregation and reference series.}
Let $\mathcal{J}_K$ denote the entry sessions with available $r^K_t$. Research returns accumulate additively. The full-market reference $r^{\mathrm{mkt}}_t$ applies the same label, exposure, and cost convention to the Shenzhen A-share daily population available to the evaluator, independently of the submitted score coverage. With $\mathcal{J}^{\cap}_K$ denoting paired strategy--reference sessions,
\begin{align}
    R^K &= \sum_{t\in\mathcal{J}_K}r^K_t,
    &\operatorname{ARR}^{K} &= \frac{243}{|\mathcal{J}_K|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{J}_K}r^K_t,\label{eq:research_arr}\\
    \Delta\operatorname{ARR}^{K} &= \frac{243}{|\mathcal{J}^{\cap}_K|}
       \sum_{t\in\mathcal{J}^{\cap}_K}(r^K_t-r^{\mathrm{mkt}}_t).
       \label{eq:excess_arr}
\end{align}
An empty required set leaves the corresponding statistic unavailable. In particular, paired excess ARR need not equal the difference of separately averaged strategy and reference ARRs when their available dates differ. An unavailable reference preserves valid strategy-only results.

ARR annualizes the mean over evaluable cohorts. Let $N_{\mathrm{exp}}$ be the number of scheduled entry sessions under the registered calendar, excluding the last accounting session; missing signals remain in this denominator. We report $n_K=|\mathcal{J}_K|$, $N_{\mathrm{exp}}$, their ratio, and cumulative research return $R^K$ beside ARR. Different observation coverage need not provide comparable calendar-period evidence, and a high ARR based on few cohorts can coexist with a small cumulative research return.

Panel-level signal, label, and paired coverage are the fractions of expected instrument--date observations with a finite signal, a valid label, and both, respectively. They differ from selected-cohort label coverage $\gamma^K_t$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:cohort_return}. Reports retain both denominators, the mean selected-cohort label coverage, and the number of valid IC days. The current daily profile defaults to zero minimum coverage ratios. Configured ratio thresholds affect statistical qualification, but financial-board eligibility does not impose a universal coverage gate. Any coverage strata used for paper comparisons are therefore a separately registered reporting policy (Section~\ref{sec:leaderboard}).

Let $P^K_j$ be the cumulative sum through the $j$-th available cohort, with $P^K_0=0$. Signed additive drawdown is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{DD}^{K}
    =\min_{0\le j\le |\mathcal{J}_K|}
       \left(P^K_j-\max_{0\le h\le j}P^K_h\right)\le0.
    \label{eq:drawdown}
\end{equation}
It is measured in return points; $100|\operatorname{DD}^{K}|$ gives its magnitude in percentage points. Cohort Sharpe is $\sqrt{243}\,\overline{r^K}/\operatorname{sd}(r^K)$ when the sample standard deviation is positive. Because adjacent cohorts overlap, this ratio is descriptive. The current primary projection does not report a portfolio information ratio.

\paragraph{Separate cash-account evidence.}
The research-return convention compares the ranking output of scoring programs under a common basket, exposure, and cost rule. It isolates this signal-selection question from cash sizing and execution constraints, while its observed-only mean reweights the remaining labels when some selected observations are missing. Cash-account evidence is needed to assess how those constraints change the result.

The evaluator also maintains a funded account with an initial value of CNY~1,000,000. The default entry allocation is the smaller of available cash and half the previous session's equity, subject to 100-share lots. Commission is three basis points with no minimum; stamp charges, transfer charges, and slippage are set to zero in this experimental profile. Morning entries cannot spend proceeds from evening exits. Missing prices, suspensions, and price-limit conditions may prevent purchases or defer sales; stale valuations and residual holdings remain recorded. These account outcomes are reported separately from Equation~\eqref{eq:cohort_return}, whose observable cohort does not require every selected security to have been funded or filled. Raw-price accounting does not incorporate corporate-action total-return adjustment, and reference-price replay does not measure live fills or market-impact capacity.

\subsection{Predictive, Efficiency, and Diversity Criteria}
\label{sec:arena_scoring}

On each valid entry date, IC is the cross-sectional Pearson correlation between $s_{i,t-1}$ and $y_{i,t}$, and RankIC is the Pearson correlation of their average ranks. Both use paired finite observations with the registered sample requirements; the current daily calculation requires at least five pairs and nonconstant inputs. Reported IC and RankIC average their valid daily values. ICIR and RankICIR divide these means by the corresponding sample standard deviations, without an additional annualization factor.

The benchmark reports financial outcomes and resource usage directly. When positive calibration targets $a_0$ and $\rho_0$ are registered, the default quality and efficiency components are
\begin{align}
    Q &= 0.7\,\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{\Delta\operatorname{ARR}^{75}}{a_0}\right)
       +0.3\,\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{\overline{\operatorname{RankIC}}}{\rho_0}\right),
       \label{eq:quality}\\
    E &= Q\max\!\left(0,1-\frac{U_{\mathrm{tok}}}{B_{\mathrm{tok}}}\right),
       \label{eq:efficiency}
\end{align}
where $U_{\mathrm{tok}}$ is total model usage when token measurements are complete; otherwise $E$ is unavailable. Diversity $D\in[0,1]$ measures qualified feature groups and their novelty relative to a frozen reference bank; its exact construction and missing-quality semantics appear in Appendix~\ref{app:diversity}. For eligible replicates with all required components available, the default composite is
\begin{equation}
    S=70Q+20E+10QD.
    \label{eq:overall}
\end{equation}
The efficiency component already contains $Q$. Calibration targets, component weights, reference policies, and qualification thresholds are frozen with the suite. The default composite requires complete token-usage evidence. CPU completeness is recorded separately; an unknown CPU measurement does not by itself suppress this score. $E$ and $D$ are score components; the platform's efficiency and diversity boards use the distinct ordering rules in Appendix~\ref{app:eligibility}.

Financial eligibility and composite-score availability are assessed separately. Arbitrary scoring programs may have available financial results while their declared feature use remains unverified, leaving diversity and a diversity-weighted composite unavailable. The main report retains all participants, identifies financial ranking eligibility, and shows composite availability and its reasons. A composite ranking describes only its qualifying subset. Episode components and scores aggregate the replicate values; a complete aggregate requires all prescribed values. Averaging only successful runs is reported explicitly as a conditional statistic. Unavailable metrics retain reason codes and sample counts, while a returned $D=0$ also requires its feature-level qualification reasons.
