\section{Conclusion}

\BenchmarkName{} evaluates a complete quantitative research run, from constructing features on raw market observations to revising candidates and committing one strategy for sealed financial assessment. A shared stock-scoring interface, portfolio rule, and cash-account evaluator connect model-authored research to comparable simulated outcomes.

Across 38 iterative configurations, the best model scores 51.3 against 77.6 for our in-house Kepler-1 agent, and only one configuration, Sol at medium effort, beats the equal-weight market. Pairing each model's best iterative and one-shot configurations, iteration raises the overall score for all nine models but sealed-period ARR for only four. Decile returns show why: most models rank stocks with some skill, yet the top-ranked names that the portfolio buys earn less than middle-ranked ones, so an average predictive correlation is insufficient to judge an investable strategy. The best-performing Astra configuration profits mainly by staying in cash during the early-2024 decline, which lowers drawdown without beating the market. Candidate trajectories show that research progress comes early and plateaus, and the effort sweep shows that a higher reasoning setting does not consistently improve the result. Together, these findings support evaluating quantitative research through both its development process and the financial outcome of its committed strategy. Repeated runs and refreshed market windows are needed to establish how reliably these outcomes generalize.
